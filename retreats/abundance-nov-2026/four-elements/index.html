THE ART OF KNOWING YOURSELF
01 / Your elemental nature

Nature needs every element to thrive. So do we.

Some people see possibilities before anyone else. Some energize a room, others sense what people need, and others create the foundations that make ideas last. These are four useful ways to reflect on your strengths, not four boxes you must fit inside.

Everyone has a mixture. Knowing what comes naturally can help you choose meaningful work, create value, collaborate well, and develop a more balanced life.

03 / Go deeper

Four elements. Many ways to flourish.

Explore the strengths, seasons, chakra associations, dosha parallels and gentle yoga practices of each element. Yoga is an invitation to explore a quality, not a method of changing your personality or treating a medical condition.

01

Spring / New beginnings

Air

The freedom to imagine what could be.

Air represents curiosity, creativity and fresh perspectives. You may be energized by ideas, exploration and looking beyond what already exists. Growth comes when inspiration becomes a small, real experiment.

SpringInnovationNew beginningsOpenness

When it serves you. Brainstorming, developing concepts, finding new solutions and envisioning possibilities.

When to balance it. Before beginning something else, complete one small step on the idea you already have.

Chakra connection. Anahata, the heart, is associated with air in a common chakra-element map. The third eye makes a useful additional metaphor for imagination, not a literal Air correspondence.

Dosha connection. Vata includes air and space. This is a symbolic parallel, not a dosha classification based on personality.

Personality research parallel. Some overlap with openness to experience in the Big Five framework.

02

Summer / Expression

Fire

The courage to connect and bring energy to life.

Fire represents enthusiasm, confidence and expression. You may thrive when rallying people, communicating ideas and inspiring momentum. Growth comes when excitement is directed toward meaningful commitments.

SummerExpressionConnectionInitiative

When it serves you. Relationship building, presenting, inspiring a team and creating shared energy.

When to balance it. Focus your enthusiasm on a few promises you can keep.

Chakra connection. Manipura, the solar plexus, is commonly paired with fire. The heart and throat can serve as additional metaphors for connection and expression.

Dosha connection. Pitta combines fire and water in Ayurvedic models. It is not equivalent to social confidence.

Personality research parallel. Some overlap with extraversion in the Big Five framework.

03

Autumn / Adaptation

Water

The wisdom to listen, care and adapt.

Water represents sensitivity to people and circumstances, patient attention and thoughtful timing. You may notice needs that others miss. Growth comes from pairing care for others with clarity about your own boundaries and direction.

AutumnCareAdaptabilityTiming

When it serves you. Deep listening, service, resolving tensions and creating trust.

When to balance it. Practise making a clear decision rather than waiting for everyone to be completely comfortable.

Chakra connection. Svadhisthana, the sacral chakra, is commonly associated with water and fluidity. The heart adds a metaphor for compassion.

Dosha connection. Water is present in both Pitta and Kapha. Nurturing and patience have a loose thematic resemblance to Kapha, but no dosha can be inferred from these traits.

Personality research parallel. Some overlap with agreeableness in the Big Five framework.

04

Winter / Foundations

Earth

The patience to build what lasts.

Earth represents steadiness, organization, practicality and follow-through. You may find satisfaction in making things work reliably. Growth comes from building helpful structure while remaining open to change.

WinterStabilitySystemsConsistency

When it serves you. Building routines, tracking progress, planning, analyzing and sustaining projects.

When to balance it. Leave room for experimentation before every detail feels certain.

Chakra connection. Muladhara, the root chakra, is commonly paired with earth and grounding. The third eye is an additional metaphor for discernment, not an Earth-element assignment.

Dosha connection. Kapha includes earth and water. Its traditional physical interpretation is not the same as being an organized person.

Personality research parallel. Some overlap with conscientiousness in the Big Five framework.

04 / Practical abundance

From stability
to abundance.

A flourishing life includes financial stability, meaningful work, health, good relationships, contribution and freedom. Think of this as a ladder of practical capabilities, not a ranking of human worth.

These pathways are illustrative, not guaranteed outcomes. Stabilizing personal finances, earning for useful work, developing a repeatable offer and collaborating with others can be relevant in different orders depending on circumstances.

Your nature is a strength.
Your next step is a choice.

Lean on your natural qualities, ask for help where you need it, and take one concrete action that creates value for yourself or others. You do not have to be an entrepreneur to live abundantly.

Further reading / Work and strengths

The Millionaire Master Plan

Roger James Hamilton

Hamilton describes four genius qualities, with different ways people create and contribute value. Our retreat loosely pairs his categories with the four elements.

Our proposed correspondences
Air and Dynamo · Ideas and creativity
Fire and Blaze · People and connection
Water and Tempo · Service and timing
Earth and Steel · Details and systems

These are retreat analogies, not traditional correspondences or mappings established by Hamilton. The ladder's Red, Orange, Yellow and Green labels are also simplified from his broader framework.

Further reading / Purpose and Ayurveda

Discover Your Dharma

Sahara Rose

Explores purpose, inner gifts and meaningful action through a modern interpretation of Ayurvedic doshas and chakra teachings. A useful companion for reflecting on how personal insight can translate into choices.

Ayurveda has five elements and three doshas, Vata, Pitta and Kapha. These do not correspond one-to-one to the four elements on this page and cannot be determined from our quiz.
